## 2026-11-26

The entries below are in the order things were found. Several of them state what was believed at
the time and was later overturned: the upscaler's clears, the colour tables, the samplers, reads
into guarded memory, the saved-settings race and the scene constants were each suspected of the
posterisation and each was cleared. The entry "Outcome of the posterisation hunt" near the end is
the one to read first; figures such as "about 1 launch in 20 is clean" are early counts (the
measured rate with ray tracing off was 15 good of 47).

- **The upscaler's clears were dropped (list slots 0x1a8/0x1b0).** A run logged `method not implemented object="command list" slot=0x1a8 caller="+0x72f4"` once: the title's AMD FSR4 upscaler DLL, in its FidelityFX backend's clear job (`SetDescriptorHeaps`, a barrier flush, then 0x1b0 or 0x1a8). Next to `ClearDepthStencilView` (0x198) and `ClearRenderTargetView` (0x1a0) these are `ClearUnorderedAccessViewUint` and `...Float` as on PC (inferred from the layout, not from the title's code). The stub answered `E_NOTIMPL`, so every such clear was lost. The upscaler's network passes synchronise through flag words at the end of a 21,045,760-byte scratch buffer bound as a raw UAV; the working hypothesis is that it resets them (or other UAV textures) with these clears each frame, and that losing them is why the final image is posterised.
- **Fix.** `crates/victory-gpu/src/list/clear.rs` (with thin versions in `list/thin.rs`): the host's clear of the same view at its place in the list, between UAV barriers. The view is made in a dynamic slot of the host heap behind the title's view heap and in a ring of a heap the card does not see. Textures are cleared in the unordered access state, depth textures through their colour twin. A buffer's range (PC memory or on the card) is refreshed and then declared written as a shader's UAV view is, so the relay lands the cleared bytes in title memory and no refresh copies older title bytes over them. Tests on WARP (`list::clear`, also with `VICTORY_THIN=1 VICTORY_RELAY=2 VICTORY_RELAY_COPY=1 VICTORY_CARD_STEPS=2` and the debug layer): cleared buffers of 4 KiB (PC copy) and 16 MiB (card copy) reach title memory with the bytes around them unchanged; textures hold Uint and Float values with and without rectangles; a depth texture holds them through its twin. `victory-gpu` suite: 347 passed, 0 failed, 34 ignored.
- **Still to confirm on screen.** Whether the posterisation goes away is not known: the game was running during the change, so no run was made with it. One diagnostics run must show `an unordered access view clear's arguments as the title passed them` (a view record at the CPU handle, a resource of ours whose size or format matches the view), `first unordered access view clear` for each method used, and the frame-rate line `UAV clears per frame: N, B bytes (R refused, ...)` with R at 0. Any `an unordered access view clear is left out` line says why one was not made.
- **Confirmed on screen, and not the posterisation.** A run with the clears showed them landing: the title's upscaler calls `ClearUnorderedAccessViewUint` once at start on its 21,045,760-byte scratch buffer and on an 80x45 texture, both to zero, with the arguments decoding as expected and none refused. The picture was still posterised. The methods were missing and are right to have; they were not the cause.
- **Posterisation is after the upscaler, in the colour-grading pass.** Consecutive-frame target dumps (frames 3500-3502) show the upscaler's HDR output (1920x1080 f10) and its history smooth. The first posterised image is the R10G10B10A2 target of `PS_CompositeNMB` (pixel `6318f669e4f17d65`, full-screen triangle; `PS_CopyDithering` `b30b0ce52e51bc0c` then copies it to the RGBA8 plane). A 34,000-pixel wall region of it holds about six colours plus the title's equal-channel dither, with steps of about 13/255 that move channels independently. That is what a 33x33x33 look-up table read without interpolation, or one whose texels repeat in blocks, gives. Ruled out by reading the translation: the composite samples three `Texture3D` tables with `SampleLevel` (x, y, z, LOD 0) through static sampler s1. The console's sampler words for it (`0x92, 0x00c00000, 0x14500000, 0`) decode to clamp addressing and filter 0x14 (linear min/mag, point mip) in `StaticSampler::from_words`. The coordinate math (log2, then a 32/33 scale and half-texel bias) matches the GFX10 code. The identity table builder (`CS_LUT3DIdentity`, `0560ac646b358460`) maps thread and group ids to texels exactly as the console code does. The three tables come from `0560ac646b358460` (identity), `497f59fd986aaebb` (tonal adjustments, a constant-buffer loop) and `f76d0645aef65f3a` (a blend of the title's placed 3D tables, 4 KiB standard swizzle, thick 8x8x8 blocks, which matches addrlib's `HwlIsThick` and `Block4K_Log2_3d`). Open: which table's contents are wrong. `capture.rs` target dumps now include 3D textures (slices side by side, texels doubled) with a log line counting equal neighbours along each axis.
- **The posterisation is per launch: about 1 launch in 20 is clean, and a launch stays either clean or posterised.** A clean capture and a posterised one from the same build differ in none of the things the ops listings show for `PS_CompositeNMB`: the same pipeline (translation size 6324, 3 static samplers), the same views, formats and slots, the same writers of every input. The 33³ and 64³ tables, the bilateral grid (30x17x32 R16G16F, its per-bin mean rising with the bin in both) and the bloom and exposure inputs are equivalent. What does differ is title data. The first two floats of the composite's shared 1392-byte post-processing constants are 0.8205127 and 0.4615384 in the clean launch, and 0.7900286 and 0.44439107 in both posterised launches. The composite does not read those two words, but they show the title computing different constants per launch. The composite runs each channel through a 4096-entry R32_FLOAT tone curve (`CS_BakeLUT_2DBezier_3Segment_LinearShaper`, `4c0ac499ad613809`, baked every frame from a 96-byte block of control points in title memory). That curve is a Texture1D, which the target dumps skipped, so it was never seen. A staircase there gives exactly the per-channel steps seen. Added for the next pair of runs: `VICTORY_CAPTURE_CONSTANTS=<hash>,...` lists every word of the constants and placed buffers those shaders read, and target dumps log 1D float tables as numbers.
- **The posterisation is quantisation in the log domain, per channel, on one global lattice.** In a posterised launch (capture21), every channel of the composite's R10G10B10A2 output, over the whole picture, falls on the same levels: about 33, 42, 53, 65 and so on (out of 255), each about 1.26 times the one before. The clean launch's histogram is smooth. Each channel snaps to the next level on its own, and all channels share the lattice. The 4096-entry tone curves are smooth in the bad launch, so they are not the cause. That pattern is what nearest-texel reads of a near-identity, log-encoded colour table give (33 entries over about 14.4 stops is about 0.45 stop a step). A wrong table would not give it. The translation reads those tables with `SampleLevel` through static sampler s1, whose console words decode to linear. The pipeline, its size and its bindings are the same in clean and bad launches, so what differs is not yet in any listing. Added logging: "shader translated" now gives a fingerprint of the DXIL plus the static samplers' console words and host filters. Ops listings give each texture's depth and levels and each view's title record. Next: diff those between a clean and a posterised launch.
- **Code review: nothing found that would bind a different sampler set from one launch to the next.**
  - Static samplers are pushed per stage with registers 0..n in one space, with distinct visibilities (vertex or mesh, then pixel; compute uses all), and the order of stages is fixed (`pipeline/host.rs` `HostPipeline::new`).
  - The composite's vertex shader has no static samplers.
  - The translator numbers immediate samplers in order of first use from a `Vec` (`lower.rs` `register_sampler`); no hash-map order is involved.
  - Pending pipelines are keyed by stage, shader hashes and graphics state (`make.rs`). Library entries are named by the root signature blob, the code and the state (`library::name`).
  - The bound root signature is skipped only when it is the same object. Identical blobs give the same object, and the list keeps every bound root signature alive.
  - The shader cache stores and restores the decoded static samplers verbatim.
  - New for the next posterised launch: `VICTORY_ROOT_LOG` logs the root signature as made and how each listed draw binds it, and the experiment `VICTORY_LINEAR_SAMPLERS` makes the listed shaders' static samplers trilinear.
- **Per-launch posterisation is title data: code review of every kernel write into title memory.** Captures 23 (good) and 24 (bad) read the composite's inputs straight from title memory: the 2048-byte scene constants at `0x956fdc200` (a double-buffered block bound some 3,000 times a frame, so written by the title's CPU each frame) hold neutral defaults at +0x17c..+0x208 in the bad launch, and the 160-byte constants hold the textbook sRGB curve (12.92, 1/2.4, 1.055, 0.055) where the good launch has a tuned one (8.13, 0.469, 1.045, 0.045). So the title computes from default settings in a bad launch. Leading suspect was a file read into a block the GPU side guards (the kernel's write fails there). Findings, by path (details in `docs/crates/victory-hle.md`, "Kernel reads into title memory"): the title and its DLLs import `ReadFile`, `fread`, `_read` (libHttpClient), `setvbuf`, `fgetc`, `std::basic_istream::read` and `DStorageGetFactory`; no `ReadFileEx`, `NtReadFile`, `MapViewOfFile`, IOCP or `DeviceIoControl`. Synchronous `ReadFile` and `fread` already opened their destination for the call and no refresh re-guards a block in `Guards::kernel` (`guard`, `protect_alias` check `left_alone`). Defects found: (1) `ReadFile` left pending dropped its `KernelWrite` at return, so a refresh could make the block clean before the bytes landed (the copies keep the old bytes; a device copying at completion would fail). (2) `ReadFile` warned only on `ERROR_NOACCESS` and never retried, though a read-only destination gives `ERROR_INVALID_USER_BUFFER` too and DirectStorage had already seen refused reads despite `kernel_writing`. (3) `_read` passed straight to the host, unwrapped. (4) `setvbuf` with a title buffer would have every stream refill written by the kernel unopened. (5) `fread` ignored its result, and its doc comment described a read-beside that does not exist. None of these was seen in runs 21-24: no `ReadFile` warning and no pending read in any of them, so none of them is shown to be the cause.
- **Fix.** `watch.rs`: `KernelWrite::keep_for` (kept ranges are left alone until a deadline) and `title_memory_guarded`. `files.rs`: `ReadFile` keeps a pending read's destination open for 2 s, retries a read refused for its buffer up to 8 times (from the OVERLAPPED offset or with the file pointer put back), and warns on every failure and on short reads the file's end does not explain; `fread` warns on a failure; `_read` is wrapped; `setvbuf` drops a title buffer so the CRT uses its own. Tests: `a_kernel_write_kept_past_its_call_stays_open_until_its_time_is_up` (fails if the write is dropped at return), `a_read_file_into_memory_guarded_for_a_moment_is_tried_again` (fails with no retries), `a_read_file_into_memory_that_stays_read_only_fails`, `an_overlapped_read_left_pending_lands` (a pipe), `descriptor_reads_and_title_stream_buffers`, `a_completion_before_its_begin_returned_is_told`.

For the first 60 s there is a `title file: first read` line per file, then a `title file: read in the first minute` summary per file (reads, bytes asked and got, failures) for `ReadFile`, `fread`, `_read` and DirectStorage. - **The unattended loop (rebuild it from this; the scripts lived in a session folder).** One PowerShell script per launch: kill any `victory.exe`; clear every `VICTORY_*` variable; set the ones `Play-VIctory.cmd` sets (parse its `if not defined X set "X=v"` lines) plus `VICTORY_LOG=info`, `VICTORY_KEYS` = Enter every 2 s from 20 s to 120 s (`20:Enter,22:Enter,...,120:Enter`, which takes the menus into the opening cutscene), `VICTORY_CAPTURE=<folder outside the repo>` and `VICTORY_CAPTURE_FRAMES` = every 50th frame from 1000 to 4600, plus the experiment's overrides; start `victory.exe run <exe> --console series-s --execute` with stderr to a log; poll the log every 2 s for `first bind ... 6318f669e4f17d65` (the composite's first use: the scene is up) and take its `frame=`; wait for the first listed frame at least 60 frames later to appear as `frame-<n>.png`; kill the game; grade that PNG; append name, tag, verdict, score, seconds, frames and overrides to a TSV. A launch takes about 90 s; run one game at a time. Grading (no third-party modules: decode the PNG with `zlib` and the five filters): for each of R, G, B take the 256-bin histogram, keep bins 12..234, skip the channel if fewer than 20 % of the pixels fall there, and sum over bins `|h[i] - mean(h[i-6..i+6])|` divided by the pixels in range; the score is the mean over the channels kept (-1 if none: too dark, UNKNOWN). Good launches score about 0.06-0.28, posterised ones 0.5-1.0; the threshold is 0.3. A person agreed with every verdict checked. Different shots of the same cutscene are graded alike, but camera-dependent constants differ between shots: compare like with like.
- **Ray tracing read zeros for most of every session: the pages for faults ran out for good (found 2026-11-26, verified in logs; effect on screen not yet confirmed).** With ray tracing on by default, the title's ray-tracing programs read title memory by raw address through the page directory (`memory.rs`), and a page a shader touches without a copy is a fault the layer fills a frame later. `take_faults` made at most 128 pages a frame and 2,048 for the whole run (`FAULTS_ALL`), and the count never went down, also when pages went back. In the logs of four launches with ray tracing on (one re-read for this entry: the `faults in all 2048` of the ten-second line is reached about 45 frames after the world appears) the per-frame WARN `shaders touched pages that had no copy` shows 550 to 1,277 pages a frame with `made=0` from then on, for the rest of the session: those reads gave 0 and those stores were dropped wherever the title's structures moved after the first second. Many of the addresses are below 4 GiB (pointers read as zero from such a page). Suspected on screen, not shown: blocky 8x8-tile shadow edges, a runaway bright hotspot, wrong glass reflections.
- **Fix.** `memory.rs` `FaultPages`: at most 128 pages made a frame and 1,024 title pages tried a frame (wild rays cannot stall a frame), at most `VICTORY_MEMORY_FAULT_MB` (new; an eighth of `VICTORY_MEMORY_CARD_MB`, 512 MiB, 8,192 pages) held at a time; past that, or when a new place would need another arena and the card has no room for one (`shadow::by_address_on_card`: under 97 % of the budget and under the arena cap), the page made longest ago is given back for it (none held fewer than 8 frames). Refusals (`capped`, `held_limit`, `no_room`) are tried again when the page faults again. Faults below 4 GiB or past the directory are counted as `wild` and spend nothing; those that are not committed or watched are `not_title` and spend a try only. A page named later by a list is held for that and leaves the count. Logged: the per-frame WARN with every count (first 40 frames, then every 100th), a ten-second INFO `pages for faults since the last line ... binding: ...`, and a WARN when every frame has been short for 5 s. Unit tests on memory of the test's own (`memory::tests`: 51,200 pages made over 400 frames with 4,096 held; the limits; the oldest first; wild faults; no room retried; the warning). Light-4's lesson (a burst of wild rays took every page in seconds) is kept by the per-frame limit and the held limit; what is new is that the held pages turn over instead of being kept for good. Not known yet: whether 8,192 pages are enough (watch `binding:` and `given_back`), and whether the visible faults go away; both need a launch.
- **Diagnostics for a runaway value: non-finite texels in dumped targets.** `capture.rs` `odd_texels`: dumped float targets (`VICTORY_DUMP_TARGETS`) count texels with a NaN, +Inf, -Inf or huge channel (at least 1e6 for 32-bit floats, the largest value for 16-bit and 11/10-bit ones) with their boxes, and each captured frame says which target in dump order is the first to have any. "target values" leaves non-finite values out of its low and high, so a NaN-filled target looked normal there.

- **Cause: 16-bit float rounding.** The accumulate pass computes its network's inputs in 16 bits (`v_cvt_f16_f32`, `v_fma_f16 2x-1`, then 48 `v_pk_fmac_f16`). - **An aliasing barrier on a committed texture ended the launch ("a host command list did not close", E_INVALIDARG).** Found while making the texture pool compact on cards of 12 GiB or less (`docs/findings.md`, 2026-11-27): both release launches with it ended in the world's load (50 s after the start) with `a host command list did not close (The parameter is incorrect. (0x80070057))`. A diagnostics build with `VICTORY_D3D12_DEBUG=1` named it: `ResourceBarrier: Committed Resource ... cannot use aliasing barrier since committed resource memory cannot be aliased`. `texture::record_upload` put an aliasing barrier before the first upload of *every* title-filled texture while the pool is on, assuming every such texture was cut from a block; one with an allocation of its own (larger than a block, or, new, made at the compact pool's cap) is refused by the host. Larger cards get it too; for them nothing changes except that a texture over 64 MiB no longer ends the launch.
